Early History of Grimes County

Family passages extracted from E.L. Blair's 1930 history — the earliest scholarly account of the families who built Grimes County, Texas
E.L. Blair · 1930 · 251 pages · Source: View full PDF →
About the Book Kennard Family Fanthorp & the Inn Womack Connection Muster Rolls Appendix I — Grantees

What this book is: E.L. Blair's Early History of Grimes County (1930) is the foundational scholarly history of the county. Blair, a graduate student at the University of Texas, spent years collecting testimony from elderly residents and descendants, cross-referencing land records, muster rolls, and newspaper archives. The result is 251 pages covering all 64 original Austin's Colony land grantees in Grimes County — with biographical sketches, family trees, and firsthand accounts that exist nowhere else in print.

Why it matters for this family: The Kennard family receives a dedicated chapter (pp. 116–125). Blair interviewed Mrs. Minnie (Kennard) Williams — a direct family member — and Judge J.G. McDonald, who knew the Kennards personally. The Womack connection, the founding of Fanthorp Inn, the firm "Fanthorp, Womack & Kennard," and the double Robertson-Womack marriage tie all appear here in primary-source form, written down within living memory of the events.

Connection to Avery: Stella Kennard (daughter of John H. Kennard, granddaughter of Michael Moore Kennard, great-granddaughter of Anthony Drew Kennard Sr.) married Gary Thomas — making her Avery's great-great-grandmother. Every person described in this chapter is Avery's direct ancestor or a close collateral relation.

The Kennard Family

Blair, pp. 116–121 · Primary source: Mrs. Minnie (Kennard) Williams, questionnaire dated January 13, 1929

Anthony Drew Kennard Sr. Direct Ancestor

b. July 9, 1788 · d. 1848, Grimes County, TX · Married Sarah "Sallie" Moore, Oct 16, 1808

Anthony Drew Kennard was granted one league of land "situated about eight miles northeast of the present town of Anderson, in Grimes county, patent to which was dated, November 22, 1832." He and his wife Sarah arrived in Texas on December 24, 1832, settling along Gibbon's Creek. The land became known as Kennard's Prairie — today Roans Prairie, TX.

"The Kennard men apparently took little interest in the political affairs of the colony, but participated actively in the military activities of the period. Anthony D. Kennard served in the Texas army on an Indian campaign in 1835, in Captain Coleman's Company, of Colonel John H. Moore's Regiment. Four of Anthony D. Kennard's sons were in the Texas army during the revolution against Mexico, namely: William E., A. D., Mark L., and Mike. M." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 117

William E. Kennard Collateral

b. July 16, 1815 · d. February 14, 1899, Cleburne, TX

William E. Kennard, eldest surviving son of Anthony D. Sr., served in the Texas Army in every major campaign of the era:

"William E. Kennard, eldest son of Anthony D. and Sarah Kennard, is said to have served in the Texas Army against the Indians in the Tidwell Campaign of 1840; as a volunteer in the San Jacinto Campaign in 1836; in the Kiell Campaign of 1839; and in the expedition against the Vasquez invasion of 1842; and was elected captain of the militia in 1841." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 119

William married a Miss Robertson — who was the sister of Mrs. A.M. Womack (Abraham Womack's wife). This means the Kennard-Womack bond ran in two directions at once: Michael Moore Kennard married Abraham Womack's daughter, while William Kennard married the sister of Abraham Womack's wife. The marriage was unhappy and ended in separation after four children: John, Rachel, Dolly, and William. The family moved to Johnson County.

Michael Moore Kennard Direct Ancestor

Son of Anthony D. Kennard Sr. · Married Mary Ann Womack

Mike M. Kennard served in the Texas army during the revolutionary period and was the driving commercial force in the family's post-independence life. He is the direct link between the Kennard and Womack families.

"Mike M. Kennard, son of the original Anthony D. Kennard, as stated above, rendered military service to Texas during the revolutionary period. He later married a Miss Womack, daughter of A. M. Womack, formerly of the Apolonia community in Grimes county. Five children were born to them, namely: Anthony D. (died while in the Southern army during the Civil War), Mary, A. W., John H., and another daughter whose name the writer does not have." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 120

Michael Moore Kennard's son John H. Kennard would go on to marry Mattie Brown and have five children including Stella Kennard — who married Gary Thomas, making her Avery's great-great-grandmother.

John H. Kennard Direct Ancestor

Son of Michael Moore Kennard · Tax collector, Grimes County
"John H. Kennard, brother of A. W. Kennard, is now a merchant at Anderson, Texas. He was tax collector of Grimes county for a term of four years, beginning in 1890. He married Mattie Brown, daughter of G. W. Brown, by whom he has five children, namely: Hiram, G. Brown, Mary Lou, Stella, and Lizzie." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 120 (footnote)

Stella Kennard — listed here as John H. Kennard's daughter — is confirmed elsewhere in Blair: "Stella is now Mrs. Gary Thomas of Anderson, Texas. She is the mother of three sons, whose names, however, the writer does not have." Those three sons include J.G. Thomas Jr., Avery's great-grandfather.

John R. Kennard Collateral

Youngest son of Anthony D. Kennard Sr. · d. 1884
"John R. Kennard, the youngest son of Anthony D. Kennard, the colonist, was too young to serve in the army of the Republic of Texas. When the Civil War came, however, he enlisted, and was promoted to the rank of Major in General Pat Cleburne's Division. At the time of his death, in 1884, he was serving as District Judge for this district, which office he had held for about four years." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 121

John R. Kennard married Sallie Nobles, daughter of Judge Nobles of Houston. They had four children: Mary, Ed, John, and Lucy.

Henry Fanthorp & The Inn

Blair, pp. 121–123 · Source: Judge J.G. McDonald to E.L. Blair, June 11, 1928

Rachel Kennard — the only daughter of Anthony D. Kennard Sr. to survive — married Henry Fanthorp in the spring of 1834, completing the house he built for the occasion. What followed was one of the most consequential institutions in early Texas history.

"Rachel Kennard, the only daughter of the elder Anthony D. Kennard, married (by bond in 1833 or 1834) Henry Fanthorp, an Englishman who came to Texas, direct from England, about 1832. Henry Fanthorp is one of the most interesting characters connected with the early history of Grimes county. He had been married twice in England. When death claimed his second wife and an only son, he determined to start life anew in the province of Texas. He arrived at Washington, Texas, sometime in the year 1832, and, after a short time, purchased from Francis Holland at a price of twenty-five cents per acre, eleven hundred and seven acres of land. He erected a log house, the ruins of which still stand, being used today by Judge J. G. McDonald as a corn crib." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 121
"In the spring of 1834, Fanthorp built a new and much larger house. This time care was taken to select a beautiful clump of trees for the site. A comfortable log dwelling with a rock chimney at each end was erected. Upon the completion of the new house, the wedding with Miss Rachel Kennard was celebrated. The couple soon found themselves in the hotel business, and Fanthorp enlarged the dwelling so as to make a spacious tavern, known as the Fanthorp Inn." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 122

The inn became the social and commercial center of the region. In 1835, it became the first post office in what is now Grimes County, with Henry Fanthorp as postmaster. In 1837, Fanthorp expanded into commerce:

"Here, in 1837, Fanthorp, with his brother-in-law, Mike M. Kennard, and Abraham Womack, established the first mercantile firm to do business in what is now Grimes county. They purchased a ten thousand dollar stock of merchandise in New Orleans, freighted the goods to Fanthorp and operated under the firm name of Fanthorp, Womack & Kennard. Here two of the most important stage lines of this section of Texas crossed, and maintained stage stands across from the tavern, where three times a week each stage stopped, watered, or exchanged teams, and made necessary repairs, while the passengers refreshed themselves at the tavern. One of these stage lines ran from Houston to Old Springfield; the other from Nacogdoches to Austin. Sam Houston was often a visitor." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 122

The inn hosted some of the most consequential figures of 19th-century America. General Zachary Taylor and his army stopped there in 1846, marching to the Mexican border. Most significantly:

"At this inn died Kenneth L. Anderson, the last Vice President of the Republic of Texas, and he was buried with Masonic honors across the road from the old tavern in the Fanthorp cemetery. When the new county was created by the First State Legislature of Texas in 1846, it was decided to name the new county seat in honor of this Kenneth L. Anderson." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 123

Henry Fanthorp and Rachel Kennard had three children: John (married Sallie Moore, died young, no children), Mary A. (married 1863 Major William M. Stone; five children including Annie B., Eleanor, Julia, William M., and Henry Fanthorp Stone), and one child who died in infancy.

The Womack Connection

Blair, pp. 119–120 · The double family bond between the Kennards and Womacks

The Kennard family was bound to the Womack family not once but twice — through two separate marriages in the same generation.

"He [William E. Kennard] married a Miss Robertson (sister of Mrs. A. M. Womack) but the marriage was an unhappy one and separation took place after the birth of four children, namely: John, Rachel, Dolly, and William." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 119
"He [Mike M. Kennard] later married a Miss Womack, daughter of A. M. Womack, formerly of the Apolonia community in Grimes county." — E.L. Blair, Early History of Grimes County, p. 120

Abraham M. Womack (b. ~July 25, 1799, Hancock, Georgia; d. ~October 19, 1861, Grimes County, TX) was therefore simultaneously the father-in-law of Michael Moore Kennard and the brother-in-law of William E. Kennard. His home community in Grimes County was Apolonia — a small settlement in the southern part of the county. He was one of three partners in "Fanthorp, Womack & Kennard," the firm that operated the first mercantile business in Grimes County history.

Abraham Womack does not appear on Blair's Appendix I list of original Austin's Colony land grantees — he arrived in Grimes County through a different route, likely a headright or preemption grant, consistent with his descendants' later Montgomery County school land patents (1905–1906).
