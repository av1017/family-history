Early History of Grimes County — E.L. Blair (1930)
The foundational scholarly history of Grimes County, Texas. Written by E.L. Blair as a graduate thesis at the University of Texas, based on interviews with elderly residents and descendants, cross-referenced with land records, muster rolls, and newspapers. Covers all 64 original Austin's Colony land grantees with biographical sketches. The Kennard family receives a dedicated chapter (pp. 116–125), including the first printed account of the Fanthorp Inn founding, the firm "Fanthorp, Womack & Kennard," and the double Kennard-Womack marriage connection. Primary source for Michael Moore Kennard's marriage to Mary Ann Womack, daughter of A.M. Womack of the Apolonia community. Blair interviewed Mrs. Minnie (Kennard) Williams — a direct family member — in January 1929.
251 pages E.L. Blair · 1930 University of Texas thesis

Thomas Family History — Louis Poole, Book 5 Chapter 19
The most authoritative document on the Thomas line. 93 pages of primary-source research by Louis Poole — himself a descendant of the family — tracing the Thomas line from progenitor Evan Thomas (~1750) through James Avery Thomas (b. ~1812) and his eight children in Grimes County, Texas. Corrects multiple published errors and family legends, including the David Thomas / Texas Declaration of Independence myth. Revised September 24, 2012.

Wheeler Family — Louis Poole, Book 5 Chapter 31
Companion chapter to the Thomas Family History. Traces the Wheeler family — whose daughter Amanda Wheeler married James Avery Thomas in 1842 — from probable North Carolina origins through Georgia and Alabama to Grimes County, Texas. Documents Henry H. Wheeler (Amanda's father), F.A.B. Wheeler (Amanda's uncle and Colonel of the Texas Militia), and the broader Wheeler family network. Debunks the "F.A.B. Wheeler first settler 1825" legend. Revised March 24, 2013.

Texas Land Grants — Original Documents

Three original land grant documents recovered from the Texas General Land Office archives (May 2026). These are the primary legal instruments by which the Thomas and Kennard family land was conveyed. All three are scanned originals — handwritten in English or Spanish on official government paper.

PDF
James Thomas — Republic of Texas Bounty Land Grant (1845)
The original Republic of Texas bounty land certificate and field notes for James Thomas, granting 320 acres in Walker County, Texas. Signed at Houston, May 28, 1838, by Charles Mason, Secretary of War. James Thomas served faithfully from June 12 to July 23, 1836 — Texas Revolution service. Survey executed January 26, 1844 by John M. Wade, Deputy Surveyor of Montgomery County. Land situated west of the San Jacinto River, beginning at the west line of the Henry Steel survey. Patent issued August 27, 1845. File 92, Abstract 558, Certificate No. 3612, Patent No. 567, Vol. 1.

William Hodge Heirs — Mexican Land Grant, State of Coahuila y Texas (1831)
Original Spanish-language Mexican land grant issued to the children of the deceased William Hodge, petitioned by his son Alexander Hodge. Written on official "Sello Tercero: Dos Reales" state paper, authorized by the State of Coahuila y Texas, 1828–29. Alexander Hodge petitioned the Commissioner on March 12, 1831 at Villa de Austin, stating his father arrived in the country in 1824 and died in 1828. Endorsed by Samuel M. Williams (Stephen F. Austin's secretary) on March 13, 1831. Land described as Sitio Número 3 above the Juntas, on the right margin of the San Jacinto River. Grant issued by Commissioner Miguel Arciniega; witnessed by Robert Taylor Jr. and C.C. Givens. Significant: Lottie Robison Crawley's maiden name note — her mother was Norma Hodge Robison, potentially connecting this line to the same Hodge family.
4 pages · Apr 24, 1831 State of Coahuila y Texas · San Jacinto District Retrieved May 2026
View →
PDF
Anthony D. Kennard — Mexican Land Grant, State of Coahuila y Texas (1832)
The original Spanish-language Mexican land grant issued to Anthony D. Kennard — one of the most significant documents in the family archive. Written on official "Sello Tercero: Dos Reales" state paper. Kennard petitioned the Commissioner on October 23, 1832 at Villa de Austin, stating he came with his family to settle and requested a sitio of land. Endorsed by Samuel M. Williams on October 24, 1832. Land described as situated east of the Río de los Brazos (Brazos River), bordering lands of Black and Hadley. Survey conducted by Horacio Chairman. Grant issued at Villa de San Felipe de Austin, November 22, 1832, signed by Commissioner Miguel Arciniega; witnessed by W.J. Foster and C.C. Givens. Abstract 34, Grimes County. Patent No. 1786, Translation No. 2104. This is the grant for what became Kennards' Prairie — later renamed Roans Prairie, which still exists along TX State Highway 90 today. 4,428.4 original acres.
4 pages · Nov 22, 1832 State of Coahuila y Texas · Grimes County Retrieved May 2026

Texas GLO Land Documents — File Wrappers & Patents

Additional land records retrieved from the Texas General Land Office GIS archives (May 2026). These are official file wrappers and patent documents — the paper jackets that enclose the original surveys, field notes, and patent certificates. Kennard and Thomas documents are in Montgomery County (adjacent to Grimes); Womack documents are school land patents from 1905–1906.

File wrapper for A.J. Kennard, File 64, Montgomery County, 1st Class grant. 8,110,530 square varas. Patented December 1, 1844, Patent No. 367, Vol. 2. A 1st Class grant indicates an original Austin's Colony colonist grant. Montgomery County bordered Grimes County — many Kennard family land holdings spanned both counties.
